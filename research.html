Research

Efficient and reliable models, from adaptation to streaming inference

My research focuses on making pretrained and foundation models easier to adapt, evaluate, and deploy when compute, data, latency, or robustness constraints matter. My current work spans parameter-efficient adaptation, causal time-series anomaly detection, and foundation-model-based visual anomaly detection.

Overview

Adaptation and evaluation under real constraints

Large pretrained models are useful because they transfer knowledge across tasks and domains, but practical adaptation still depends on how much state must be trained, how much compute and memory are available, how distribution shift changes behavior, and how predictions are aligned with the evidence available at inference time. I study these questions through efficient adaptation methods and anomaly-detection settings where evaluation details are especially important.

01 · Adaptation

Efficient foundation-model adaptation

My current direction studies parameter-efficient fine-tuning and low-rank adaptation, including LoRA/QLoRA, adapter-based fine-tuning, low-rank approximation, SVD-based model compression, and efficient attention. I am particularly interested in when low-rank structure is sufficient, how rank and update structure should be chosen, and how efficiency can be improved without treating model quality as an afterthought.

This is an active research direction and technical study area; I do not present it as a completed LLM publication.

02 · Streaming

Causal time-series anomaly detection

Streaming anomaly detection must distinguish when a score becomes available from the timestamp to which that score is attributed. My work studies causal evaluation, sliding-window inference, bounded attribution delay, and reproducible protocols that avoid hidden look-ahead. The goal is to make online anomaly-detection claims correspond to the information a detector actually had at decision time.

03 · Vision

Visual anomaly detection

I work with CLIP- and ViT-based anomaly-detection methods, focusing on reliable reproduction, robustness, test-time behavior, and image- and pixel-level evaluation. I have reproduced and benchmarked WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, APRIL-GAN, and AF-CLIP across MVTec AD, VisA, and MVTec LOCO to build a stronger experimental foundation for new research.

04 · Research practice

Reproducible machine-learning research

Many apparent model improvements disappear when preprocessing, checkpoint selection, evaluation alignment, or implementation details change. I therefore treat reproducibility as part of the research method: experiment automation, result aggregation, baseline reproduction, ablations, dependency and GPU-environment debugging, and explicit evaluation protocols are central to how I work.
