Research

Efficient and reliable adaptation of foundation models

My work is organized around one broad question: how can pretrained models adapt to new tasks and changing conditions while keeping compute, latency, data, and reliability constraints explicit?

Current direction

Efficient LLM adaptation

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning, LoRA/QLoRA, low-rank approximation, adapter-based learning, model compression, and efficient attention. I am particularly interested in methods that connect practical efficiency with a clear understanding of optimization behavior.

Active research

Streaming time-series anomaly detection

Causal inference and evaluation for anomaly detection, including delayed attribution, bounded detection latency, sliding-window score alignment, and reproducible evaluation under online constraints.

Foundation models for vision

Visual anomaly detection

CLIP- and ViT-based anomaly detection, test-time adaptation, robustness, and careful image- and pixel-level evaluation across MVTec AD, VisA, and MVTec LOCO.

Methodology

Reproducible ML research

Experiment automation, baseline reproduction, ablation design, model/checkpoint debugging, result aggregation, and evaluation pipelines that make it easier to distinguish method behavior from implementation artifacts.

Research trajectory

My current MSc work began with visual anomaly detection, expanded into causal time-series anomaly detection, and is now extending toward efficient foundation-model adaptation and low-rank methods for large language models. I want these areas to remain connected through the common problem of efficient, reliable adaptation.
